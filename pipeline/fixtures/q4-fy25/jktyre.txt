MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY
Replacement demand firmed up through the March quarter as fleet utilisation recovered after
a subdued festive period, and off-highway volumes benefited from a better rabi season; the
original equipment channel, however, remained soft in medium and heavy commercial vehicles,
so consolidated revenue grew only modestly over the December quarter. Realisations were
broadly flat, the pricing actions taken earlier in the year having been largely absorbed by
discounting in passenger car radials.

The margin compression of the last three quarters continued. Natural rubber remained the
principal drag, with domestic RSS-4 averaging close to a fifth above the March 2024 quarter,
while carbon black and synthetic rubber tracked crude higher and imported feedstock landed
costlier on a weaker rupee; gross margin was some 340 basis points below the corresponding
quarter of the previous year. Cost of materials consumed at Rs. 2,099.08 Crore was 58.1% of
sales against 54.6% a year earlier.

Capital expenditure of Rs. 214.60 Crore in the quarter — Rs. 688 Crore for the full year —
went principally to the passenger car radial line at Banmore, the mixing capacity at
Chennai and to solar and biomass installations across the Kankroli and Mysuru plants. Gross
borrowings were reduced by Rs. 145.80 Crore during the quarter and the Company continues to
target a debt-equity ratio below 1.0 by the close of FY26.

The results were reviewed by the Audit Committee and approved by the Board of Directors at
its meeting held on 8th May, 2025. The statutory auditors have audited these results and
have issued an unmodified opinion thereon.
